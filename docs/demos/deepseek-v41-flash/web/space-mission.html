Mission Objective

Why we go

Ares Reach is not just a mission — it's a statement. We're pushing the boundaries of human endurance and robotic capability to establish a sustainable presence beyond Earth orbit.

The primary objective of Ares Reach is to deploy the Olympus Habitat, a modular research station designed to support a crew of four for up to 18 months in Mars orbit. The station will serve as a proving ground for technologies critical to future surface missions.

The crew will conduct over 120 scientific experiments spanning astrobiology, materials science, human physiology, and planetary geology. Data collected will inform the design of the first crewed Mars surface mission, currently planned for the 2030s.

This mission represents a collaboration between NASA, ESA, JAXA, and commercial partners, with contributions from 14 nations. It is the most complex international space endeavor since the International Space Station.

Spacecraft

Olympus Habitat

A next-generation deep-space habitat designed for long-duration missions. The Olympus combines proven life-support systems with advanced radiation shielding and autonomous navigation.

Built for the long haul

The Olympus Habitat is a 12-meter-long, 8-meter-diameter cylindrical module with a pressurized volume of 320 cubic meters. It features a dual-layer radiation shield, regenerative life-support systems, and a docking port compatible with both Orion and Starship vehicles.

  • Length 12.4 m
  • Diameter 8.2 m
  • Pressurized Volume 320 m³
  • Solar Array Output 45 kW
  • Radiation Shielding 20 g/cm²
  • Crew Capacity 4 (nominal)
Mission Timeline

From launch to landing

Every phase of Ares Reach is meticulously planned. Here's how the mission unfolds over its 18-month duration.

T-00:00:00 — Launch
Liftoff from LC-39A
Falcon Heavy lifts off from Kennedy Space Center, carrying the Olympus Habitat and the Ares Reach crew. Max Q at T+72 seconds, booster separation at T+2:30.
T+00:08:30 — Orbit Insertion
Low Earth Orbit Checkout
The spacecraft enters a 400 km parking orbit. Systems are checked, solar arrays deploy, and the crew performs a 24-hour systems validation before the trans-Mars injection burn.
T+00:36:00 — Trans-Mars Injection
Departure Burn
A 22-minute burn of the main engine sends the spacecraft on a 7-month trajectory to Mars. The crew begins their long-duration spaceflight protocol.
T+07:12:00 — Mars Orbit Insertion
Arrival at Mars
The spacecraft performs a critical orbit insertion burn, placing the Olympus Habitat into a 400 km circular Mars orbit. The crew begins their 18-month research mission.
T+25:00:00 — Return to Earth
Mission Complete
After 18 months of groundbreaking science, the crew departs Mars orbit for the 7-month journey home. Splashdown in the Pacific Ocean, completing the most ambitious deep-space mission in history.
